THE PROCESS

01

From physical reference to digital geometry

Model the optical engine

I began by sketching and modeling a supplied 3D-printed optical module at full scale in Fusion 360. The module houses the laser, camera, and light source; its geometry establishes the packaging constraints for the surrounding device.

Deliverables: a fully dimensioned manufacturing drawing, a rendered image, and an animated view of the module.

02

A medical device with an approachable presence

Explore the form

A mood board guided the visual direction toward a non-intimidating device for procedures performed while the patient is awake. I explored multiple hand-sketched concepts, then developed the selected direction in three views.

Design priorities: human scale, left- or right-handed use, and a form that accommodates the optical engine and fluid controls.

03

Turn the sketch into a full-scale assembly

Package the components

The next stage translated the selected sketch into a shelled, split housing. The layout needed to secure the optical module, accommodate a 24F sheath, and reserve space for fluid connections, valve actuation, and electrical routing.

Key decision: balance the external form with usable internal space and a practical assembly layout.

04

Design for fit, retention, and assembly

Resolve the interfaces

Detailed CAD development addressed how the parts connect: housing fixings, mating features, valve-control mounts, sheath retention, and retained luer-lock connections. The brief also called for internal water pipes and a three-pole electrical connection or hardwire with strain relief.

Assembly focus: separate component bodies, appropriate mating tolerances, and secure interfaces between the housing and its internals.

05

Preserve design intent while simplifying production

Refine for manufacture

The manufacturing review focused on adapting the housing for injection molding without losing the original design intent. Draft, part alignment, component retention, sink risk, and housing stiffness were considered alongside assembly access and finished quality.

Review focus: explain the geometry changes that support molding and assembly, and illustrate the tradeoffs behind them.

06

Use simulation to guide the next iteration

Evaluate structural behavior

The structural-analysis assignment called for meshing and constraining the assembly, applying a load case, and identifying stress or displacement hotspots. The recommended case applies a moment at the handle with the sheath tip fixed, followed by a geometry change and a repeat simulation.

Evidence to include: the actual load and constraints, material assumptions, mesh, and comparable before-and-after results. Quantitative improvement should be reported only once confirmed.

07

Make the external form and internal logic clear

Communicate the design

The final presentation brings together exterior renders, internal views, and an animation. These visuals communicate the device form, component arrangement, and the design revisions developed through feedback and manufacturing review.

Presentation focus: show both the finished appearance and the engineering decisions inside the housing.

DESIGN TAKEAWAY

The enclosure is
part of a system.

This project connects external form with internal packaging, assembly, and production constraints. The case study follows those decisions from the optical module to a complete device concept, with simulation and visualization providing ways to evaluate and explain the design.

Scope: an academic design study. This page documents the design process; it does not establish clinical performance or regulatory approval.
